> *Remark*. A measure assigns a size to each set in a [collection of sets](sets.llms.md#def-collection): how many elements it has, how long it is, or how likely it is. These notes build measures from \\\sigma\\-algebras and additivity, using the definitions on the [Sets](sets.llms.md) and [Functions](functions.llms.md) pages. One kind of measure, the probability measure ([Definition 12 in Measures and Probability Measures](measures-measure-spaces.llms.md#def-probability-measure)), is the starting point of the Morrison Lab’s probability notes ([probability measure](https://morrison-lab.github.io/pds/probability-basics.html#def-probability)).

The measure notes are split across these pages:

- [Sigma-Algebras](measures-sigma-algebras.llms.md): \\\sigma\\-algebras, generated \\\sigma\\-algebras, and the Borel \\\sigma\\-algebra.
- [Additivity](measures-additivity.llms.md): finite and countable additivity.
- [Measures and Probability Measures](measures-measure-spaces.llms.md): measures, the counting and Lebesgue measures, measure spaces, null sets, \\\sigma\\-finite measures, probability measures, and probability spaces.
- [Measurable Functions and Integrals](measures-integrals.llms.md): measurable functions, the integral, and absolutely integrable functions.
- [\\L^p\\ Spaces](measures-lp-spaces.llms.md): \\L^p\\ norms and spaces, the essential supremum, and the Hölder and Minkowski inequalities.
- [Product Measures](measures-product.llms.md): product \\\sigma\\-algebras and product measures.

A link to a section, theorem or definition on this page from before the split goes on to the page that now holds it.

## 1 Further reading

- Billingsley [\[1\]](#ref-billingsley1995probability) develops measure theory and builds probability on it. Its early chapters define \\\sigma\\-algebras, measures, and their basic properties.
- Folland [\[2\]](#ref-folland1999real) is a graduate text on measure and integration; its first chapters treat \\\sigma\\-algebras and measures.
- Gut [\[3\]](#ref-gut2013) is a graduate course in probability that opens with a chapter on introductory measure theory.
