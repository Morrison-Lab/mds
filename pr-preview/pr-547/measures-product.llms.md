## 0.1 Product measures

> **NOTE:**
>
> **Definition 1 (Measurable rectangle)** Let \\(S, \mathcal{S})\\ and \\(T, \mathcal{T})\\ be [measurable spaces](measures-measure-spaces.llms.md#def-measurable-space). A **measurable rectangle** is a [Cartesian product](sets.llms.md#def-cartesian-product) \\A \times B\\ with \\A \in \mathcal{S}\\ and \\B \in \mathcal{T}\\.

> **NOTE:**
>
> **Definition 2 (Product \\\sigma\\-algebra)** For the measurable spaces \\(S, \mathcal{S})\\ and \\(T, \mathcal{T})\\ of [Definition 1](#def-measurable-rectangle), the **product \\\sigma\\-algebra** \\\mathcal{S}\otimes \mathcal{T}\\ is the \\\sigma\\-algebra on \\S \times T\\ [generated by](measures-sigma-algebras.llms.md#def-generated-sigma-algebra) the measurable rectangles.

> **NOTE:**
>
> **Example 1 (Product \\\sigma\\-algebras)**  
>
> - Give the die rolls \\D\\ the \\\sigma\\-algebra \\\mathcal{S}\\ of all its subsets. Each single pair \\{\mathopen{}\left\\(a, b)\right\\\mathclose{}} = {\mathopen{}\left\\a\right\\\mathclose{}} \times {\mathopen{}\left\\b\right\\\mathclose{}}\\ in \\D \times D\\ is a measurable rectangle. Every subset of \\D \times D\\ is a union of finitely many single pairs, and \\\mathcal{S}\otimes \mathcal{S}\\ contains finite unions of its sets ([Theorem 1 in Sigma-Algebras](measures-sigma-algebras.llms.md#thm-sigma-algebra-closure)), as well as \\\emptyset\\, so \\\mathcal{S}\otimes \mathcal{S}\\ is the collection of all subsets of \\D \times D\\.
> - The rectangle \\\[0, 2\] \times \[0, 3\]\\ is a measurable rectangle for the Borel \\\sigma\\-algebras, so it is in \\\mathcal{B}\otimes \mathcal{B}\\.

> **NOTE:**
>
> **Definition 3 (Product measure)** Let \\(S, \mathcal{S}, \mu)\\ and \\(T, \mathcal{T}, \nu)\\ be measure spaces with [\\\sigma\\-finite](measures-measure-spaces.llms.md#def-sigma-finite) measures \\\mu\\ and \\\nu\\. The **product measure** \\\mu\otimes \nu\\ is the measure on the [product \\\sigma\\-algebra](#def-product-sigma-algebra) \\\mathcal{S}\otimes \mathcal{T}\\ that gives each measurable rectangle the product of the measures of its sides:
>
> \\(\mu\otimes \nu)(A \times B) = \mu(A) \\ \nu(B) \quad \text{for all } A \in \mathcal{S}\text{ and } B \in \mathcal{T},\\
>
> with \\0 \cdot \infty = \infty \cdot 0 \stackrel{\text{def}}{=}0\\ and \\x \cdot \infty = \infty \cdot x \stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\infty\\ for \\x \> 0\\. Exactly one measure has these values [\[1\]](#ref-billingsley1995probability), Theorem 18.2.

> **NOTE:**
>
> **Example 2 (Product measures)**  
>
> - For the counting measure \\\mu\\ on the die rolls \\D\\, \\(\mu\otimes \mu)(A \times B) = \mathopen{}\left\|A\right\|\mathclose{} \mathopen{}\left\|B\right\|\mathclose{}\\, the number of pairs in \\A \times B\\. For example, the set of pairs in which both rolls are even is \\{\mathopen{}\left\\2, 4, 6\right\\\mathclose{}} \times {\mathopen{}\left\\2, 4, 6\right\\\mathclose{}}\\, with
>
>   \\ \begin{aligned} (\mu\otimes \mu)({\mathopen{}\left\\2, 4, 6\right\\\mathclose{}} \times {\mathopen{}\left\\2, 4, 6\right\\\mathclose{}}) &= 3 \cdot 3 \\ &= 9 \end{aligned} \\
>
>   pairs.
>
> - For Lebesgue measure \\\lambda\\ on \\\mathbb{R}\\,
>
>   \\ \begin{aligned} (\lambda\otimes \lambda)(\[0, 2\] \times \[0, 3\]) &= 2 \cdot 3 \\ &= 6, \end{aligned} \\
>
>   the area of the rectangle.
